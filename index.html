Reading the sky

Every star here is a cell.

A single-cell atlas looks a lot like a night sky. Each point is one cell; cells with similar expression gather into clusters, and we name the clusters the way astronomers name constellations.

Development runs one way: a distal-tip progenitor settles into an AT2 cell. My PhD asks whether the trip can be made in reverse — reprogramming an adult AT2 cell back toward that progenitor state — and which controls move it, in what order. Charting that arc is where the bench and the model meet.

scElixir — a pipeline that refuses to print a number it has not computed

Six stages take an adult cell state back toward its embryonic progenitor — atlas → gene-regulatory network → in-silico reversal → TF recipe → small-molecule mirror → deep-learning fate validation. Reference case: adult AT2 → fetal distal-tip progenitor. Phase 1 ships as one self-contained HTML portal.

iREP — reprogramming lung stem cells back up the landscape

I run computational and experimental studies of the epigenetic reprogramming of alveolar type 2 cells into expandable distal lung progenitors — a lineage-specific inducible reprogramming (iREP) mouse model, patient tissue, and single-cell multiomics to map the epigenetic and transcription-factor changes that drive the fate switch.

An oligo-PROTAC degrader of C/EBPα

C/EBPα is a master transcription factor with no drug-shaped pocket. So the molecule borrows what the target already binds: a short double-stranded DNA that mimics C/EBPα's genomic site, tethered to a handle that recruits the cell's own protein-disposal machinery. Two spatial atlases, rebuilt from what was actually deposited

Mouse lung development — from raw reads

The source study deposited only raw sequencing (GSA CRA014010 — 13 runs, 471 GB of FASTQ), so every layer was recomputed for eight sections spanning E12.5 → P3 and packaged as one offline portal. A phantom barcode well had to be found and corrected first; after QC one section yields 1,581 spots on tissue against the 1,580 the paper reports. CellChat adds 132 pathways across 15 populations. Underlying paper: Chen et al. 2025.

Human fetal lung — Visium + Xenium viewer

GW15 and GW18, two Visium FFPE and two Xenium sections (862,798 and 980,282 cells), with every annotation re-derived from public GEO deposits. Held-out accuracy is ≈ 0.71 for fine cell types and 0.94 for compartments; low-confidence cells stay Unassigned (~17%). Underlying paper: Quach, Farrell et al. 2024.

Honest labels. Personal public-data projects, local only — not published work. Cell-population labels are my own, the mouse H&E layer is computed pseudo-H&E, and nothing was substituted for data the authors never deposited.

Publications

Five things now known.

  1. 01

    Development of Oligo-PROTACs That Target C/EBPα

    Cai J, Qi X, Smiley AT, Brownfield DG

    Makes a transcription factor long written off as undruggable addressable, using its own DNA-binding preference as the targeting device.

    ACS Chem. Biol.2026 · online firstFirst author · 1st of 4Closed access · cover feature
    Summary & figures

    C/EBPα is a master transcription factor that decides cell identity and acts as a tumour suppressor, but it has no drug-shaped pocket, so conventional inhibitors have never worked on it. This paper builds a different kind of molecule: a short piece of double-stranded DNA that mimics the stretch of genome C/EBPα normally grabs, chemically tethered to a handle that recruits the cell's own protein-disposal machinery. In two cell lines the best of six designs reached the nucleus and cleared endogenous C/EBPα at sub-micromolar doses without obvious toxicity, and the cells' gene expression shifted in the direction you would expect if C/EBPα had been removed.

    Cai J, Qi X, Smiley AT, Brownfield DG. ACS Chem. Biol. 2026 (online first). Cover artwork by the author, ACS Chemical Biology, Sept 2026.
    https://doi.org/10.1021/acschembio.6c00462

    From Cai J, Qi X, Smiley AT, Brownfield DG. ACS Chem. Biol. 2026, doi:10.1021/acschembio.6c00462 — author's own figure; reuse subject to publisher permission

  2. 02

    A molecular circuit regulates fate plasticity in emerging and adult AT2 cells

    Sawhney AS, Deskin BJ, Cai J, et al., Brownfield DG

    Identifies the molecular timer that opens and closes the lung's capacity to regenerate — and shows it is re-used during repair.

    Nat. Commun. 16:892420253rd of 22 authorsOpen · CC BY-NC-ND 4.0
    Summary & figures

    Working in the developing mouse lung, the authors catch the moment individual alveolar type 2 cells first appear: they pop up in the middle stretches of a growing branch rather than at its tip, push out of the epithelial sheet, and bridge to a neighbouring airspace. For a while those new cells can still change their mind about what to become, and the window closes when the transcription factor C/EBPα shuts down Notch signalling through a previously unknown regulatory switch near Dlk1; a polycomb complex controls both genes, producing a timed pulse that scatters type 1 and type 2 fates in a salt-and-pepper pattern. In injured adult lungs the same logic runs in reverse — C/EBPα levels have to drop before type 2 cells regain that flexibility, and losing C/EBPα also unlocks a separate defensive cell state distinct from the repairing one.

    Sawhney AS, Deskin BJ, Cai J, et al., Brownfield DG. Nat. Commun. 2025;16:8924.
    https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-64224-1

    Fig. 1 from Sawhney et al., Nat. Commun. 2025;16:8924, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 · Fig. 7 from Sawhney et al., Nat. Commun. 2025;16:8924, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 — both shown whole and unaltered.

  3. 03

    The roles of TGF-β, Wnt, and MAPK signaling pathways in joint lineage specification in vitro and ex vivo

    Raj S, Cutia T, Menghini S, Alemany-Ribes M, Cai J, et al., Craft AM

    A recipe and reporter system for making joint-lineage cells from stem cells — a prerequisite for engineering joint repair tissue.

    Stem Cell Reports 20:10268520255th of 9 authorsOpen · CC BY 4.0
    Summary & figures

    To study how a synovial joint first gets specified, the team engineered a mouse embryonic stem cell line carrying two fluorescent readouts — one for Gdf5, an early joint marker, and one for Prg4, the lubricating protein of joint surfaces — so joint-like cells light up as they appear in culture. TGF-β signalling turned out to be both necessary and sufficient to switch those reporters on, while damping down either Wnt or MAPK signalling pushed many more cells into the joint programme and turning either pathway up blocked it. Single-cell sequencing placed the resulting cells alongside cartilage-forming cells from real embryonic limb buds, and the same pathway manipulations reproduced the effect in cultured limb bud tissue.

    Raj S, Cutia T, Menghini S, Alemany-Ribes M, Cai J, et al., Craft AM. Stem Cell Reports 2025;20(11):102685.
    https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stemcr.2025.102685

    Fig. 1 from Raj et al., Stem Cell Reports 2025;20:102685, CC BY 4.0 · Fig. 4 from Raj et al., Stem Cell Reports 2025;20:102685, CC BY 4.0

  4. 04

    A conserved transcription factor regulatory program promotes tendon fate

    Niu X, Melendez D, Raj S, Cai J, et al., Galloway JL

    Names a conserved two-factor programme for making tendon cells on purpose — it matters because tendon injuries heal badly.

    Dev. Cell 59:310620244th of 14 authorsClosed access
    Summary

    Hunting for signals that make tendon cells, the authors ran a large chemical screen in zebrafish and found forskolin reliably pushes cells toward tendon identity; it works through the transcription factor Creb1a, which they show is both required and sufficient for that fate. They then found matching regulatory DNA elements in human, mouse and fish that switch on specifically in tendons, and the sequence motifs inside them pointed to a second family of factors, the EBFs — mutating either motif broke the element's tendon specificity, and zebrafish lacking ebf1a/ebf3a formed tendons poorly. Supplying both factors together nudged cells toward tendon in zebrafish and in human pluripotent stem cells.

    Niu X, Melendez D, Raj S, Cai J, et al., Galloway JL. Dev. Cell 2024;59(23):3106–3123.e12. Closed access — no figures reproduced here.
    https://doi.org/10.1016/j.devcel.2024.08.006

  5. 05

    Current Prevention of COVID-19: Natural Products and Herbal Medicine

    Huang J, Tao G, Liu J, Cai J, Huang Z, Chen JX

    A reference list of natural compounds with reported anti-coronavirus activity — a review of candidates, not evidence of prevention.

    Front. Pharmacol. 11:58850820204th of 6 authors · reviewOpen · CC BY 4.0
    Summary & figures

    Written in the first year of the pandemic, this review looks at the prevention side of COVID-19 rather than treatment, at a point when vaccine candidates were still in trials and long-term efficacy data did not exist. It gathers the natural compounds reported to inhibit human coronaviruses and the herbal formulations that were being used clinically during the outbreak, giving particular attention to preparations that already had regulatory approval and identifiable patents. The authors argue that orally stable, cheaply manufactured natural products were worth evaluating as stop-gap prophylactics for the window before a proven vaccine was widely available.

    Huang J, Tao G, Liu J, Cai J, Huang Z, Chen JX. Front. Pharmacol. 2020;11:588508.
    https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2020.588508
